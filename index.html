I studied engineering.
I think like a designer.

I'm a creative designer and a Computer and Communication Engineering graduate from Abubakar Tafawa Balewa University. Somewhere between lectures I started a YouTube channel — and that turned into the most useful education I've had.

I grew that channel from zero to 50,000+ subscribers. Doing it meant learning every part of the thing myself, right down to the website that had to sit behind it. Nobody handed me a template. I learned by watching things fail and working out exactly which part lost the person on the other end.

"Every skill I sell, I learned because something of my own wasn't working and I refused to leave it broken."

That's the through-line in both things I do now. A website and an app are the same problem at different scales: someone arrives with an intention, and every screen either moves them forward or gives them a reason to leave. The engineering side keeps me honest about performance and structure; the design side keeps me honest about whether any of it is actually pleasant to use.

Today I design and build websites and apps for creators, businesses, and organisations — from a landing page to a full product. I work directly with clients: you talk to the person doing the work, and you hear back fast.

If you care more about whether a thing performs than whether it wins a design award, we'll get along fine.
